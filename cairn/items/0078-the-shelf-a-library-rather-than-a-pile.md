A thousand fonts on disk is not a library; it is a pile with a search box. The thing
that makes it a library is that somebody has made decisions about it, and those
decisions survive.

Collections exist and are excellent plumbing: ordered, named sets that export to JSON
and travel with their fonts. They are also a subcommand with six of its own
subcommands, which is to say they are a feature rather than the shape of the product.

This milestone makes the shelf the thing you are looking at. What you reach for, what
you are trying out, what a project needs, what you have ruled out and why. Sets you
assemble by acting rather than by naming; sets that show you what is in them at a glance
rather than as a count; sets you can hand to somebody else and have them open the same
library you closed.

The freedom clause is load-bearing here rather than decorative. A shelf you can only
open with fontina is not yours — so every set stays a plain file, every export is
readable without this program, and deleting the binary leaves the decisions behind.
