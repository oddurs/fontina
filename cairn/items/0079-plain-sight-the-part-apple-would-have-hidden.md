Everything above is the Apple half: decide the default, show one obvious action, get
out of the way. This is the half that keeps it honest, and it exists because the failure
mode of good product design is a machine that is pleasant and opaque.

Three things are never hidden.

**What it did.** Every action fontina takes on your behalf names itself, prints the
command that would have done the same thing, and can be undone. A font manager writes to
your system; a font manager you cannot audit is a font manager you have to trust.

**What a font lets you do.** Freedom is not a filter tucked behind `--free`. Whether you
may study, change and pass on a face is a fact about it as basic as its weight, and it
belongs where the weight is. `OS/2.fsType` stays reported and never enforced — it is the
file's assertion about itself, not a term of any licence, and acting on it would mean
restricting you on your own computer.

**Where it came from.** Which directory, which file, which of the four copies of Inter
you have, and what the program did to it and when.

And the clause the rest of the roadmap is measured against: **nothing the interface can
do is unavailable to a script.** The product may decide the default; it may never remove
the lever.
